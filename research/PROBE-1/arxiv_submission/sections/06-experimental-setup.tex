\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}

\subsection{Physics Simulation Testbed}
All empirical evaluations are conducted in **Isaac Lab v2.3** powered by Isaac Sim 5.1 \cite{isaacsim2024}. Rigid-body dynamics and contact mechanics are computed using PhysX 5 with continuous collision detection (CCD) at an internal numerical integration frequency of $1\text{ kHz}$ ($\Delta t_{\text{phys}} = 1.0\text{ ms}$). High-speed optical tactile interaction is simulated via the TacSL module, generating elastomeric surface displacement vectors, shear strain fields, and Poisson marker flow across four fingertip contact pads. The outer policy and safety filter operate at $100\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 10\text{ ms}$).

\subsection{Operational Regimes}
We evaluate controllers across four distinct operational regimes designed to isolate and combine primary failure modes in physical grasping:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Regime A (Quasi-Static Step Friction Collapse):} Object mass is held constant ($m_o = 0.40\text{ kg}$). At step $30$ ($t = 0.30\text{ s}$), surface friction collapses abruptly by $50\%$ (from $\mu = 0.60$ to $\mu = 0.30$), testing transient shear adaptation.
    \item \textbf{Regime B (Dynamic Liquid Influx):} Surface friction is constant ($\mu = 0.60$). A fluid stream pours continuously into the vessel at a rate of $+0.25\text{ kg/s}$, increasing container mass from $0.40\text{ kg}$ to $0.525\text{ kg}$ over 50 steps.
    \item \textbf{Regime C (Center-of-Mass Sloshing):} Object mass is constant, but dynamic liquid sloshing induces sinusoidal migration of the Center of Mass: $\mathbf{c}_o(t) = [0.03 \sin(2\pi \cdot 2.5 t), 0, 0]^T\text{ m}$, generating continuous gravitational tilting moments.
    \item \textbf{Regime D (Fully Coupled Nonstationary):} The compound stress benchmark: continuous fluid influx ($+0.25\text{ kg/s}$) + periodic CoM slosh ($\pm 3.0\text{ cm}$) + \textbf{abrupt 60\% friction collapse} (from $\mu = 0.60$ to $\mu = 0.24$) triggered at step $30$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baseline Suite}
We compare HOROS PROBE-1 against four baseline paradigms:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{B1: Fixed Force Policy:} Dispatches constant joint torque commands ($u = 0$), maintaining an open-loop pre-grasp normal force ($f_N = 4.0\text{ N}$).
    \item \textbf{B2: Reactive Slip Squeeze Reflex:} Monitors the local friction-utilization ratio $S_i = \frac{\|\mathbf{f}_{T, i}\|}{\hat{\mu}_i f_{N, i}}$. When $S_i > 0.85$, it commands reflexive joint clamping ($u_i = 12.0\text{ rad/s}^2$).
    \item \textbf{B3: Unconstrained Flow Policy:} A generative policy commanding nominal task-space tracking accelerations ($u_i = 0.8\text{ rad/s}^2$) without safety barrier constraints.
    \item \textbf{B4: Deterministic Certainty-Equivalence MPC:} Receding-horizon controller tracking normal force based on instantaneous point estimates $\hat{\mu}_i$, but omitting conformal uncertainty margins ($k_{\text{margin}} = 0$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Statistical Evaluation Protocol}
To satisfy peer-review reproducibility standards:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Every condition evaluates $N = 100$ independent trials with unique pseudo-random seeds ($seed_i = seed_{\text{base}} + i$).
    \item Competing controllers share bitwise identical initial conditions and disturbance trajectories.
    \item Binary success/failure outcomes (Drop and Crush rates) are evaluated via paired McNemar tests with Edwards continuity correction \cite{mcnemar1947note}, and 95\% confidence intervals are reported using the Wilson score method.
    \item Continuous performance metrics (survival steps, force tracking) are compared via paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests \cite{wilcoxon1945individual}, Cliff's Delta ($\delta$) \cite{cliffs1993dominance}, and Matched-Pairs Rank-Biserial correlation ($r_{\text{rb}}$). All hypothesis tests enforce Holm-Bonferroni multi-comparison correction at significance level $\alpha = 0.01$.
\end{itemize}
