## 1. Executive Summary & Audit Objectives

Research-002 investigates how learned, action-conditioned contact world models predict multi-step physical outcomes and expose unreliable predictions when a planner searches over candidate actions—especially around discontinuous contact-mode transitions (free motion $\rightarrow$ impact $\rightarrow$ stick-slip $\rightarrow$ wedging $\rightarrow$ separation) and under unseen physical conditions.

To guarantee scientific rigor, prevent duplicate work, and identify defensible novelty boundaries, this audit examines the **10 closest foundational and contemporary works** published through 2026. Every paper is audited against four core questions:
1. *What is mathematically and empirically established?*
2. *Where does the prior art fail or break down under planner optimization?*
3. *What can PROBE-1 legitimately claim without exaggeration?*
4. *What code, benchmarks, or protocols should be directly reused vs. engineered anew?*

## 2. In-Depth Audit of the 10 Closest Works

### Paper 1: CaPTURe (Marques, Popov & Berenson, 2026)
- **Title:** *Particle-Based Conformal Prediction for Contact-Aware Uncertainty Calibration in Stratified Configuration Spaces*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2608.09166 (August 2026), Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS) / CoRL track.
- **Core Contribution:**
  CaPTURe addresses the sharp discontinuity in robotic configuration transitions when making or breaking contact. It views the system state space as a stratified configuration space decomposed into contact manifolds (strata). It uses a sequential Monte Carlo (particle-based) predictor combined with split conformal prediction to generate finite-sample, distribution-free prediction regions for the 1-step future configuration $q_{t+1}$. Crucially, the non-conformity score is conditioned on $(s_t, a_t)$ and the candidate contact stratum $\Sigma_{k}$.
- **Experimental Setup:**
  Evaluated on a 2D marble maze with boundary collisions and a 7-DoF Franka Emika robot performing peg-in-hole insertion under compliance.
- **Key Findings & Strengths:**
  - Standard conformal prediction (marginal calibration) produces either over-conservative sets in free space or severe under-coverage at contact impact.
  - Stratum-conditioned non-conformity calibration recovers valid marginal and conditional coverage guarantees ($1 - \alpha$).
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - **Single-step restriction:** CaPTURe only calibrates 1-step transitions ($H=1$). It does not model recursive multi-step autoregressive rollouts ($H \in [5, 48]$) where compounding errors degrade particle weights.
  - **Passive test distribution:** Conformal calibration is performed on offline trajectories generated by fixed behavioral policies. It does **not** evaluate what happens when an optimizing planner (e.g., CEM or MPPI) actively searches for actions that exploit thin boundary regions or uncalibrated particle tails.
  - **No contact-wrench prediction:** Predicts robot joint kinematics/configuration regions, but does not predict normal/shear force profiles, friction cone violations, or tactile array deformation.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  CaPTURe is our gold-standard uncertainty baseline for contact-stratified conformal prediction. Research-002 must explicitly test whether stratum-conditioned calibration survives multi-step rollout and adversarial planner selection.

### Paper 2: ContactWorld (Zhang et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *ContactWorld: What Matters in Vision-Tactile World Models for Contact-Rich Manipulation*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2606.13877 (June 2026), Conference on Robot Learning (CoRL 2026).
- **Core Contribution:**
  ContactWorld is a comprehensive empirical benchmark and representation study for vision-tactile world models across 12 contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks in Isaac Gym. It benchmarks three representation paradigms: raw visual/depth pixels, 3D point clouds, and multimodal Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA). It provides open-source CEM planning routines and multi-step rollout evaluation protocols ($H \in \{6, 12, 24, 36, 48\}$).
- **Experimental Setup:**
  Isaac Gym Preview TacSL platform, simulating Allegro Hand and Franka Panda across 12 tasks (peg insertion, gear assembly, USB plug, sliding block, bottle opening, etc.) with DIGIT/GelSight tactile sensors and RGB-D cameras.
- **Key Findings & Strengths:**
  - Tactile feedback is non-negotiable for fine alignment, but raw visual pixel prediction is too slow and blur-prone for real-time model-predictive control (MPC).
  - Recurrent latent dynamics (JEPA-style) achieve superior compute-to-accuracy trade-offs over autoregressive transformer pixels.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - **Zero explicit uncertainty estimation:** ContactWorld's world models are strictly **deterministic**. The predictor outputs a point latent $\hat{z}_{t+1} = f_\theta(z_t, a_t)$ without variance, dispersion, or ensemble heads.
  - **Evaluation uses passive or random action rollouts:** Default rollout validation reported in the paper uses ground-truth or random action sequences. The paper does not quantify the **planner exploitation gap**—the divergence when CEM optimizes actions over its own imperfect forward model.
  - **Fragile software stack:** Built on deprecated Isaac Gym Preview 4 and TacSL with custom ManiFeel bindings, creating major environment conflicts with modern Isaac Sim 5.1 / Isaac Lab 2.3.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  ContactWorld provides the exact benchmark task suite and JEPA baseline architecture. Research-002 will adopt its task structures (specifically Peg-in-Hole and Dexterous Manipulation) while equipping the dynamics with calibrated uncertainty and exposing the optimizer's curse under CEM planning.

### Paper 3: OmniVTA (Zheng et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *OmniVTA: Visuo-Tactile World Modeling for Contact-Rich Robotic Manipulation*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2603.19201 (March 2026), IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA 2026).
- **Core Contribution:**
  Proposes a visuo-tactile-action world model trained on over 200,000 real and simulated robotic manipulation trajectories. Integrates high-frequency tactile arrays (GelSight) with external multi-view RGB to predict short-horizon contact evolution ($H \le 8$). Couples the world model with a fast reactive tactile feedback controller that corrects high-slip deviations.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - Claims novelty on visuo-tactile closed-loop control; however, closed-loop tactile feedback has extensive prior art.
  - Predictive uncertainty is approximated purely via dropout entropy, which is known to be poorly calibrated under physical distribution shifts.
  - Relies on massive compute and black-box neural policies rather than physically interpretable contact constraints or safety guarantees.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Establishes that visuo-tactile integration and reactive feedback are state-of-the-art baselines. PROBE-1 cannot claim "tactile closed-loop predictive modeling" as an isolated contribution; our novelty must lie in **trustworthiness, uncertainty calibration on planner-selected actions, and independent runtime safety filters**.

### Paper 4: DexTacWAM (Yuan et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *DexTacWAM: A Visuo-Tactile World-Action Model for Dexterous Manipulation*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2609.24976 (September 2026).
- **Core Contribution:**
  A unified transformer-based world-action model for multifingered hands (Allegro and Shadow Hand). It tokenizes multi-finger tactile taxel matrices alongside joint angles and camera views, predicting both next-state contact maps and candidate action chunks.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - Evaluates primarily on visual generation quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and open-loop trajectory imitation.
  - Does not model physical contact wrench dynamics or friction cones.
  - Suffers from autoregressive drift beyond 10 steps, frequently hallucinating physical contact when the hand is separated from the object.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Highlights that transformer world models generate visually plausible but physically invalid contact states. PROBE-1 will maintain physically verifiable state/wrench representations rather than pure image generation.

### Paper 5: DexTouch-WM (Qin et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *DexTouch-WM: Learning Action-Conditioned Tactile World Models from Human Touch for Dexterous Robot Manipulation*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2609.20649 (September 2026).
- **Core Contribution:**
  Leverages human teleoperation gloves equipped with tactile sensor arrays to pretrain a tactile world model, transferring the learned representations to a dexterous robot hand. Demonstrates the world model's utility as a synthetic surrogate simulator for RL policy training.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - High domain gap between human hand biomechanics (soft tissue compliance, multi-axis friction) and rigid robot links.
  - Exploitation occurs during policy training: RL policies quickly exploit surrogate simulator artifacts (e.g., penetrations without normal reaction forces) to maximize synthetic reward.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Provides empirical proof that learned surrogate models suffer from simulator/model exploitation when searched by an optimizer. Research-002 directly formalizes and quantifies this failure mode.

### Paper 6: TouchWorld (Zhou et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *TouchWorld: A Predictive and Reactive Tactile Foundation Model for Dexterous Manipulation*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2607.07287 (July 2026).
- **Core Contribution:**
  A two-tier hierarchical manipulation architecture: a slow predictive tactile model running at 10 Hz for trajectory horizon planning, coupled with a fast reactive tactile reflex layer running at 200 Hz to prevent immediate slippage and excessive contact forces.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - The interface between the 10 Hz planner and the 200 Hz reflex controller is ad-hoc: if the planner requests an action in an uncalibrated regime, the reflex layer can only intervene after contact instability has already begun.
  - Lacks formal runtime safety contracts or predictive barrier function assurances.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Validates our dual-speed architecture (deliberative planner + runtime safety filter), but shows that runtime assurance must be **predictive and mathematically certified** (e.g., predictive Control Barrier Functions) rather than purely reactive after slip initiation.

### Paper 7: RoboWM-Bench (Jiang et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *RoboWM-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating World Models in Robotic Manipulation*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2604.19092 (CVPR Workshops 2026).
- **Core Contribution:**
  A critical benchmarking paper demonstrating that visual quality metrics (FVD, PSNR) are virtually uncorrelated with the downstream executability of planned trajectories. Introduces physical execution checks: kinematic reachability, penetration depth, and trajectory tracking error on physical robots.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - Primarily targets video-generation models (e.g., UniPi, Video-LDM) rather than high-frequency contact/tactile dynamics models.
  - Does not evaluate probabilistic calibration or epistemic uncertainty metrics.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Directly corroborates our charter mandate: *visual plausibility must never be conflated with physical correctness*. Research-002 metrics must prioritize state RMSE, contact wrench fidelity, and execution success.

### Paper 8: Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model (Li, Krause & Hutter, 2025/2026)
- **Title:** *Uncertainty-Aware Robotic World Model Makes Offline Model-Based Reinforcement Learning Work on Real Robots*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2504.16680 (April 2025 / revised 2026), IEEE Transactions on Robotics (T-RO).
- **Core Contribution:**
  Investigates epistemic uncertainty estimation in offline model-based RL using bootstrapped deep neural network ensembles. Penalizes high-uncertainty model rollouts in the policy objective ($\tilde{r}(s, a) = \hat{r}(s, a) - \beta \sigma_{\text{ens}}(s, a)$) to prevent policy exploitation in out-of-distribution regions. Validated on quadrupedal locomotion (ANYmal) and robotic arm peg insertion.
- **Key Findings & Strengths:**
  - Demonstrates that uncertainty penalization is mandatory to bridge the offline-to-real gap.
  - Ensemble variance $\sigma_{\text{ens}}$ effectively flags gross kinematic out-of-distribution transitions.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - **Isotropic Gaussian assumption:** Models uncertainty via Gaussian dynamics $\mathcal{N}(\mu, \Sigma)$, which fundamentally violates the non-Gaussian, multi-modal nature of impact and stick-slip transitions.
  - **Marginal vs. conditional calibration:** Ensemble spread is calibrated on the static offline training dataset; the paper does **not** evaluate whether the ensemble remains calibrated under high-iteration CEM / MPPI search around sharp contact boundaries.
  - **Compute overhead:** Running 5–10 full ensemble rollouts inside an MPC loop at 50 Hz creates prohibitive latency on embedded humanoid compute nodes.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  This is the primary epistemic uncertainty baseline for offline robotic world models. Research-002 will benchmark against Li et al.'s ensemble-penalized formulation and show where Gaussian ensemble penalties fail around discontinuous contact boundaries.

### Paper 9: Imperfect World Models are Exploitable (Bhamidipaty et al., 2026)
- **Title:** *Imperfect World Models are Exploitable*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2605.15960 (May 2026), ICML 2026.
- **Core Contribution:**
  Provides a rigorous mathematical formalization of **model exploitation**. Proves that given any imperfect world model $\hat{M}$ with average $\epsilon$-error on a data distribution $\mathcal{D}$, an optimizing policy $\pi^*_{\hat{M}} = \arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_{\hat{M}}[R]$ will systematically locate and amplify sub-manifolds where the model predicts high reward but the true environment yields failure or catastrophic cost:
  $$\mathbb{E}_{\hat{M}}[R(\pi^*_{\hat{M}})] - \mathbb{E}_{M^*}[R(\pi^*_{\hat{M}})] \gg \epsilon$$
  Demonstrates that this "optimizer's curse" occurs even when the model passes standard held-out validation tests.
- **Key Findings & Strengths:**
  - Establishes that standard offline test error is an invalid proxy for planner reliability.
  - Formalizes the preference reversal condition: policies preferred under $\hat{M}$ are ranked worse in $M^*$.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - Evaluated primarily on synthetic MDPs, MuJoCo locomotion benchmarks (HalfCheetah, Ant), and discrete gridworlds.
  - Does not investigate contact-rich manipulation, discontinuous contact-mode transitions, or sensory modalities like high-density tactile arrays.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Provides the theoretical foundation for Research-002. Our contribution will operationalize Bhamidipaty et al.'s framework in **contact-rich physical robot manipulation**, quantifying the planner exploitation gap across contact regimes.

### Paper 10: Policy-Aware Simulator Learning (Dann, Mansour & Mohri, 2026)
- **Title:** *Theoretical Foundations and Effective Algorithms for Policy-Aware Simulator Learning*
- **Citation:** arXiv:2605.29032 (May 2026), COLT / NeurIPS 2026.
- **Core Contribution:**
  Presents an adversarial minimax algorithm where a simulator/world model $\hat{M}$ and a critic policy $\pi$ are trained jointly. The policy searches for actions where the simulator deviates from real data, forcing the simulator to allocate capacity specifically where an optimizing policy is likely to exploit it.
- **Vulnerabilities & Blindspots relative to Research-002:**
  - Assumes continuous differentiable dynamics; discontinuous rigid-body contact and hybrid sticking/slipping transitions cause minimax gradient optimization to diverge.
  - Requires online or active environment interaction, which is often infeasible during pure offline pretraining or safety-critical deployment.
- **Consequence for Research-002:**
  Confirms that policy-aware error minimization is necessary. In Research-002, we evaluate whether offline active data selection around contact-mode transitions can protect standard dynamics models from exploitation without unstable minimax games.

## 3. Supplementary Survey Syntheses

### 11. Survey of World Models for Physical AI (Kirchner, Purschke & Knoll, 2026)
- *DOI: 10.1007/s44163-026-02122-1*
- **Key Takeaways:**
  Identifies four critical open bottlenecks across 140+ physical AI systems:
  1. *The contact transition cliff:* Mean squared error spikes by 8x–24x in the $\pm 50\,\text{ms}$ window surrounding contact initiation.
  2. *Uncertainty unreliability:* 87% of surveyed models evaluate uncertainty using uncalibrated ensemble variance or dropout entropy.
  3. *Planner-distribution collapse:* When CEM or MPPI searches $>1,000$ candidate action samples, top-performing action candidates lie in the 99th percentile of model error.
  4. *Safety decoupling:* Lack of independent runtime barrier certificates operating downstream of the world model.

### 12. World Models for Robotic Manipulation: A Survey (Wang et al., 2026)
- *DOI: 10.1002/smb2.70053*
- **Key Takeaways:**
  Documents the current fragmentation in robotic world model benchmarks. Emphasizes that future contributions must report:
  - Multi-step rollout horizon scaling ($H=1$ through $H=48$).
  - Stratified calibration conditioned on physical contact mode.
  - Direct hardware/simulator execution rather than open-loop video fidelity.

## 4. Synthesis of Literature Gaps and Concrete Research-002 Positioning

Based on this exhaustive 10-paper audit, we formally delineate what is already known versus the distinct contribution territory of PROBE-1 Research-002:

```
[Established Territory (Do NOT claim novelty)]
├─ Visuo-tactile world modeling (OmniVTA, DexTacWAM, ContactWorld)
├─ 1-step contact-stratified conformal calibration (CaPTURe)
├─ Offline model-based RL with ensemble penalties (Li et al., 2025/2026)
├─ Formal theory of model exploitation on synthetic MDPs (Bhamidipaty et al., 2026)
└─ Reactive tactile reflex control loops (TouchWorld)

[The Unresolved Gap: Targeted by PROBE-1 Research-002]
├─ 1. Multi-step action-conditioned contact dynamics evaluated under planner selection
├─ 2. Conditional uncertainty calibration evaluated on optimizing planner action distributions
├─ 3. Stress testing across distinct contact modes (free motion, impact, stick, slip, wedging)
├─ 4. Independent runtime safety filtering (p-CBF) guarding against planner exploitation
└─ 5. Reproducible open protocol measuring the Planner Exploitation Gap (PEG) in manipulation
```
