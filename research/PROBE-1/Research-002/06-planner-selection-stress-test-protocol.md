## 1. Experimental Motivation: The Optimizer's Curse in Contact World Models

Standard evaluation protocols in robotic world modeling report aggregate multi-step prediction error (RMSE, cosine similarity, FVD) on held-out test datasets generated by passive behavioral policies:
$$\text{RMSE}_{\text{passive}} = \sqrt{\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{s}, \mathbf{a}) \sim \mathcal{D}_{\text{behavior}}}\left[ \|\mathbf{s}_{t+1} - \hat{\mathbf{s}}_{t+1}\|_2^2 \right]}$$

However, deploying a world model in a Model-Predictive Control (MPC) loop introduces an active search process:
$$\mathbf{a}^* = \arg\max_{\mathbf{a}_{t:t+H-1}} \mathbb{E}_{\hat{M}}\left[ \sum_{k=0}^{H-1} r(\hat{\mathbf{s}}_{t+k}, \mathbf{a}_{t+k}) \right]$$

When an optimizer (e.g., CEM or MPPI) samples $N=1,000$ candidate trajectories and selects the top-ranked actions, it systematically exploits regions where the learned world model $\hat{M}$ is overconfident or physically uncalibrated. This produces the **Planner Exploitation Gap (PEG)**:
$$\operatorname{PEG}(\pi_{\text{opt}}) = \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\tau} \sim \pi_{\text{opt}}^{\hat{M}}}\left[ J_{\hat{M}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}) \right] - \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\tau} \sim \pi_{\text{opt}}^{\hat{M}}}\left[ J_{\text{Real}}(\boldsymbol{\tau}) \right]$$

## 3. Evaluated Failure Modes & Hypotheses

1. **Hypothesis 1 (Optimizer's Curse on Deterministic Models):** Deterministic models (B1) will exhibit low passive RMSE on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{behavior}}$, but severe PEG ($\text{PEG} > 50$) and high safety violation rates ($>50\%$) under CEM search because the planner exploits unpenalized non-physical penetration shortcuts.
2. **Hypothesis 2 (Ensemble Uncertainty Breakdown under Contact Discontinuity):** Isotropic Gaussian deep ensembles (B3) will dampen gross kinematic drift, but fail to detect wedging and slip bifurcations, leaving an exploitation gap under physical OOD conditions.
3. **Hypothesis 3 (Independent Runtime Assurance):** The independent Predictive Control Barrier Function (p-CBF) filter will decouple safety from world model exploitation, enforcing certified zero crush/jam violations without sacrificing task completion.
