# HOROS PROBE-1 — Contact World-Model Prior-Art Matrix

**Document:** Research Novelty Gate, deliverable 2  
**Date:** 2026-11-19  
**Rule:** Pre-contact prediction, pre-slip prediction, incipient-slip detection, post-slip detection, and post-failure recovery are different events. A paper that does one does not do the others.

Timing vocabulary used below:

| Term | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pre-contact | The decision is issued before the robot makes the contact that could fail. |
| Pre-slip | The decision is issued before the first sample that a declared slip sensor would call slip. |
| Incipient slip | A precursor on a tactile image or deformation field, which may itself be the first observable slip signal. |
| Post-slip | The decision uses a slip measurement that has already crossed the declared threshold. |
| Post-failure recovery | Action after an observed failure or after an abstention caused by that observation. |

## 3. Active probing and system identification

This gate did not find a paper that is identical to the candidate HOROS protocol. That absence is not novelty. The occupied neighboring claims are:

- ContactGuard already conditions a failure score on the proposed action and aborts before contact. `[PAPER]`
- Classical and learned tactile methods already treat deformation before gross slip as informative. `[ABSTRACT]`
- Online friction estimation and gentle grasping are listed as occupied in the HOROS October memo under claim C3. `[REPO]`
- Active excitation is the standard way to identify a parameter that is silent under a passive observation. The HOROS Phase 3.7.2 report already states the empirical version of that fact for Regime A: steps before the friction change are not separable from in-distribution behavior. `[REPO]`

## 4. What none of the retrieved papers demonstrates

The following conjunction was not found in the retrieved texts:

1. a hidden friction change that leaves the current observation statistically unchanged,
2. a **safe** action-conditioned probe that stays below the slip threshold,
3. a forecast, issued before the first sample labelled as slip, of whether a later task action will slip,
4. a separate estimate of friction and of mass,
5. a calibration curve for that pre-slip score,
6. an intervention that begins before irreversible slip and is not only an abort of gripper closure.

Missing that conjunction from this reading list does not prove it is absent from the literature. It does identify the only comparison a HOROS experiment would have to beat.
