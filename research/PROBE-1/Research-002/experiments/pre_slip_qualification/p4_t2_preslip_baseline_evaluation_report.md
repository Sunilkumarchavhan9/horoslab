## 2. Mathematical Formulation & Physical Mechanisms

### 2.1 The Observable Precursor in Contact Physics v2
In legacy Simulator v1 (`CompliantPreLiftContactEnv`), tangential contact followed an idealized Amontons-Coulomb law coupled to an invariant linear spring:
$$F_t = k_{\text{pad}} \delta_t, \quad k_{\text{pad}} = 1500\text{ N/m} = \text{const}$$
Under v1, $\partial F_t / \partial \mu = 0$ everywhere on the sticking branch, and gross slip occurred as an unobservable step discontinuity without precursor ($\tau_{\text{lead}} \equiv 0\text{ ms}$).

In **Contact Physics v2** (Cattaneo-Mindlin continuum mechanics), the contact disc between an elastomeric fingertip ($R = 15\text{ mm}$, $E_1 = 5\text{ MPa}$) and workpiece decomposes into an inner sticking core ($0 \le r \le c$) and an expanding outer annular micro-slip region ($c < r \le a$):
$$\frac{c(t)}{a(t)} = \left( 1 - \frac{|F_t(t)|}{\mu_s F_n(t)} \right)^{1/3}$$

The instantaneous tangential stiffness $k_t = dF_t / d\delta_t$ softens monotonically as shear load increases:
$$k_t(F_t) = k_{t,0} \cdot \left( \frac{c}{a} \right) = k_{t,0} \left( 1 - \frac{|F_t|}{\mu_s F_n} \right)^{1/3}$$
where $k_{t,0} = 8 G^* a$ is Mindlin's initial tangential stiffness.

As gross slip approaches ($|F_t| \to \mu_s F_n$), $c \to 0$ and $k_t \to 0$. This continuous loss of tangent stiffness broadcasts the proximity to slip **before** macroscopic sliding occurs!

```
 Continuum Traction and Softening Evolution
 ==========================================
 1.0 |=========\
     |          \   <--- STIFFNESS SOFTENING: kt / kt0 = c / a
 0.6 |           \       Encodes proximity to gross slip!
     |            \
 0.2 |             \
 0.0 +------+-------+-------+-------+---> Ft / (mu * Fn)
    0.0    0.4     0.7     0.9     1.0 (Gross Slip)
```

### 4.4 Frame-Level Discrimination & AUROC

Frame-level classification was evaluated between the pre-slip window ($c/a \le 0.60$) and the safe sticking regime ($c/a \ge 0.80$):

| Metric | Baseline 1 (Force Ratio) | Baseline 2 (Stiffness Softening) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **AUROC (Clean Signals)** | **$0.9897$** | **$0.6155$** |
| **AUROC (Noisy Signals)** | **$0.9897$** | **$0.5952$** |

#### Why AUROC and Lead Time Show Divergent Trends:
- **Baseline 1 AUROC ($0.9897$):** Within any *single* trajectory where friction is constant, force ratio increases monotonically. When all frames are pooled, high loads separate well from zero loads. However, as demonstrated in Section 4.3, a single static threshold *cannot* operate across varying $\mu_s$: any threshold that detects low-friction slip produces massive false alarms on high-friction slip, making Baseline 1 practically unusable without knowing $\mu_s$ a priori.
- **Baseline 2 AUROC ($0.6155$):** Finite-difference differentiation ($dF_t / d\delta_t$) introduces gradient variance, particularly during early micro-sticking and transient dynamic loading. However, Baseline 2 is **dimensionless and universal**: it tracks the true physical contraction of the stick core ($c/a$) across all friction levels without requiring prior knowledge of $\mu_s$.

## 5. Technical Insights for P4-T3 Model Development

The P4-T2 baseline evaluation yields three critical scientific insights that directly govern the architecture of **P4-T3**:

1. **Stiffness Softening is the Physical Invariant:**  
   Unlike raw forces or force ratios which scale directly with $\mu_s$, the normalized stiffness degradation $\bar{k}_t = c/a$ is a universal physical precursor. P4-T3 world models should predict the dynamic grasp stability margin $M_t = 1 - |F_t|/(\mu_s F_n) = (c/a)^3$ rather than attempting raw force thresholding.
2. **Noise Amplification Mandates Temporal Sequence Modeling:**  
   Simple finite differences on raw sensor telemetry suffer from high-frequency noise ($\sigma_F = 0.02\text{ N}$). At low velocities, $\Delta \delta_t$ is on the order of micrometers, magnifying noise in $\Delta F_t / \Delta \delta_t$. This proves why deep temporal sequence models (TCN / GRU / Transformer in P4-T3) and Kalman/RLS compliance estimators are essential to filter high-frequency vibrotactile noise and extract clean stiffness gradients.
3. **Epistemic Uncertainty is Required to Suppress False Alarms:**  
   Sensor noise causes premature threshold crossings during safe sticking (e.g., $31.3\%$ false alarm rate at $\theta = 0.40$). Integrating conformal uncertainty prediction (B3/B4 conformal thresholds) in P4-T3 will enforce rigorous coverage bounds ($1 - \alpha = 0.95$) to guarantee zero false alarms during normal pre-lift manipulation.
